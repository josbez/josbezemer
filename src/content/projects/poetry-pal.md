## The Challenge

Poetry Pal set out to use AI to match readers with poems based on mood and conversation. The open question wasn't whether the AI worked, it was whether AI should be the product's center of gravity at all, or a feature inside something else.

## My Role

I led UX research and concepting for the app's chat and recommendation feature, owned the product backlog, and ran and analysed the user testing sessions that ended up answering that bigger question.

## Approach

I opened the project with a five-day kickoff sprint that ended in a client presentation, then built the complete product backlog myself: 72 tickets across nine work streams, from the poem bundle and chat to community features and the festival stand, with the open conceptual questions, is this a bundle or an anthology, what should the tone of voice be, what should even be measurable, written down as tickets rather than left implicit so nobody could quietly skip them. Testing surfaced friction around chat, discovery, and personalization, but the more useful signal came from five separate rounds of validation across three methods: one-on-one interviews, field research with real visitors during a poetry festival, and a survey. All three consistently pointed to the same reframing: people talked about the app not as an "AI tool" but as a way into poetry. I held the AI itself to the same design rigor as the interface, writing known failure modes, hallucinated chat replies, mismatched languages between a poem and its preview, no profanity filter, no load testing under concurrent use, into the backlog as explicit requirements before they could surface as complaints.

## Impact

The conclusion reframed the product: AI stopped being the centerpiece and became something that served what the app fundamentally is, a poetry archive. That reframing changed how the recommendation feature was prioritized going forward, and it settled a months-long internal debate over the product's basic shape, bundle or anthology, that got reopened mid-project when the research disagreed with the earlier answer, and was then closed for good.

## Learnings

The most valuable output of a research session isn't always the answer to the question you asked, it's sometimes a correction to the question itself.
