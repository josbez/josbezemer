## The Challenge

Oorlogsbronnen.nl exists to make Dutch WWII history searchable across dozens of independently run archives, museums, and heritage organizations. The hard part was never the content, it was structure: every partner organization had its own definitions of what counts as a "person," an "announcement," or a "source," and the platform had to hold all of that together without flattening it into something meaningless.

## My Role

I joined in 2020 as a reviewing interaction designer, and became the de facto product owner of the studio's side of the project from 2021, the person partners work with directly when their collection needs to connect to the platform. That meant running the workshops myself, not just attending them: on the map module, on oral history, on how announcements ("bekendmakingen") should be modeled.

## Approach

Early on, funding earmarked for static infographics turned out to serve the wrong goal. I worked with the client to redirect that scope toward something more useful: integrating individual life stories into the platform instead, a reframing that set the direction for the next two years and fed directly into the relaunched platform that went live in November 2021. When the platform later migrated from Drupal to Sanity, I treated the redesign as a structural reset rather than a like-for-like rebuild: homepage, article pages, and 760,000 renewed person pages were rebuilt around shared definitions that all partners could agree to, not just the loudest one. In 2025 I also designed an entirely new page type for the network's thematic "specials," built as a set of optional, freely orderable components rather than a fixed template, so editors could assemble a themed story without needing a new template each time; that page type now hosts the network's Kunstroof (art theft) theme, live since March 2026. And when transcripts and fragments from oral-history interviews started being generated by AI in 2026, I treated the transparency of that content as its own design question rather than an implementation detail: how a visitor can tell what's AI-generated, and trust it anyway.

## Impact

The partnership has run for six years and is still active, most recently on an oral-history feature and a full remediation of a 97-item accessibility audit, including a practical fix for its hardest finding: giving data visualizations a text alternative through an info button instead of a wall of text. That longevity is the real measure of success here: a multi-stakeholder structure that partners keep choosing to extend rather than work around.

## Learnings

The clearest lesson from this project: in a multi-organization platform, the structure only holds if every partner had a hand in shaping it. Workshops aren't a formality, they're where the real design work happens.
