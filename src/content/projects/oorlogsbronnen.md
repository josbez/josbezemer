## The Challenge

Oorlogsbronnen.nl exists to make Dutch WWII history searchable across dozens of independently run archives, museums, and heritage organizations. The hard part was never the content, it was structure: every partner organization had its own definitions of what counts as a "person," an "announcement," or a "source," and the platform had to hold all of that together without flattening it into something meaningless.

## My Role

I've been the UX/information designer for the WO2NET network since 2021, the person partners work with directly when their collection needs to connect to the platform. That meant running the workshops myself, not just attending them: on the map module, on oral history, on how announcements ("bekendmakingen") should be modeled.

## Approach

When the platform migrated from Drupal to Sanity, I treated the redesign as a structural reset rather than a like-for-like rebuild: homepage, article pages, and person pages were rebuilt around shared definitions that all partners could agree to, not just the loudest one. That meant slower consensus-building up front, in exchange for a model that wouldn't need renegotiating with every new partner.

## Impact

The partnership has run for five years and is still active, with new workshops (including one on AI, planned for WO2NET's 2026 network day) still being scheduled. That longevity is the real measure of success here: a multi-stakeholder structure that partners keep choosing to extend rather than work around.

## Learnings

The clearest lesson from this project: in a multi-organization platform, the structure only holds if every partner had a hand in shaping it. Workshops aren't a formality, they're where the real design work happens.
