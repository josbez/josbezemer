## The Challenge

The CABR archive contains the case files from the post-war prosecution of collaboration, real names, real accusations, some still legally sensitive today. Oorlog voor de Rechter had to make this searchable for both researchers and the public, while the data itself is only partially public since 2025. The challenge wasn't a search interface, it was deciding what "open" should even mean here.

## My Role

I facilitated the project's kickoff between the Nationaal Archief, Huygens Institute, NIOD, WO2NET, and Spinque, and owned the design of how people search, browse, and move through dossiers and legal cases: the layer where the privacy/openness tension actually surfaces to a user. As the minimal viable version moved toward launch, that shifted into interaction design on specific pieces of it, accessibility, and the documentation the archive's own editors still work from.

## Approach

Every review with the consortium came back to the same question: whose interest wins in this specific screen, the researcher's need to cross-reference, or the subject's right not to be casually surfaced? I designed the search results and dossier navigation to make that distinction visible rather than hide it behind a generic search box. At kickoff, a full day with all five institutions, I collected and consolidated every partner's presentation into one shared board so the discovery phase had a single source of truth instead of five separate ones. Later, working toward the minimal viable version, I worked through microcopy like screen-reader labelling directly with the archive's own team rather than deciding it in isolation.

## Impact

The minimal viable version went live at the Nationaal Archief on 1 July 2025. I also drafted the project's formal accessibility statement, and chose to be upfront that it would score "does not comply" without an independent audit, rather than overstate what had actually been verified. That statement, and the CMS manual written for the archive's editors, are still the reference documents the project runs on. Internally, this was one of the two projects that challenged me most, precisely because the stakes were real, not hypothetical. Getting a multi-institutional consortium to agree on where those lines sit is itself a form of impact that doesn't show up in a metric.

## Learnings

Sensitive data doesn't need a more careful interface, it needs the design process itself to slow down and include the people who understand the stakes. The consortium wasn't overhead, it was the safeguard. And being honest about what a product doesn't yet comply with is itself a design decision, not an admission of failure.
