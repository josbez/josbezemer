## The Challenge

The CABR archive contains the case files from the post-war prosecution of collaboration, real names, real accusations, some still legally sensitive today. Oorlog voor de Rechter had to make this searchable for both researchers and the public, while the data itself is only partially public since 2025. The challenge wasn't a search interface, it was deciding what "open" should even mean here.

## My Role

I facilitated the project's kickoff between the Nationaal Archief, Huygens Institute, NIOD, WO2NET, and Spinque, and owned the design of how people search, browse, and move through dossiers and legal cases: the layer where the privacy/openness tension actually surfaces to a user.

## Approach

Every review with the consortium came back to the same question: whose interest wins in this specific screen, the researcher's need to cross-reference, or the subject's right not to be casually surfaced? I designed the search results and dossier navigation to make that distinction visible rather than hide it behind a generic search box.

## Impact

Internally, this was one of the two projects (alongside Spacebuzz) that challenged me most in 2024, precisely because the stakes were real, not hypothetical. Getting a multi-institutional consortium to agree on where those lines sit is itself a form of impact that doesn't show up in a metric.

## Learnings

Sensitive data doesn't need a more careful interface, it needs the design process itself to slow down and include the people who understand the stakes. The consortium wasn't overhead, it was the safeguard.
