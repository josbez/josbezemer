## The Challenge

Oorlog voor de Rechter is the digital portal for the archives of the Centraal Archief Bijzondere Rechtspleging (CABR), one of the most fraught archives in the Netherlands: case files from the post-war prosecution of Dutch citizens *suspected* of collaboration with the Nazis. It's a collaboration between the Nationaal Archief, Huygens Institute, WO2NET, and data-search partner Spinque.

The rule that shaped everything: the platform was never allowed to pass judgement on a person. The archive can be incomplete, and there are variables it doesn't capture, so the risk of being wrong, and of that being consequential for someone's name, was too high. Every design decision had to hold that line.

## My Role

As UX designer, I was responsible for the UX design and logic of how people search, browse, and understand dossiers and legal cases.

I facilitated all of Norday's workshops and the project's kickoff with all parties and stakeholders, presented our progress at demos, worked with a strategist on the underlying strategy, translated that into interaction design, and worked closely with development to see it through. Being responsible for UX, I helped set up user testing, conducted by the team from Huygens Institute, and visited the archive in person to understand the material as thoroughly as possible.

## Approach

Working from regular reviews with the consortium, I designed the UX of the whole platform: search, search results, and the logic behind the case/dossier summary and full search. Always with the same tension in mind: this is sensitive personal data, only partially public since 2025, but the historical and public interest in accessing it is significant.

## Impact

Internally, this was one of the two projects (alongside Spacebuzz) that challenged me the most in 2024, precisely because the stakes, privacy versus openness, were real, not hypothetical.

## Learnings

The clearest constraint on this project wasn't technical, it was ethical: never let the design imply a verdict the archive itself couldn't support. That single rule shaped the search logic and interaction design more than any deadline did.
